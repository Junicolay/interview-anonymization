# Research Question

## Current research question

How can speaker-identifying information in interviews be reduced through anonymization methods while preserving semantic content?

## Possible alternative formulations

- Which anonymization strategies are most effective at reducing speaker-identifying information while preserving the original interview content?

- How can acoustic and linguistic characteristics of an interview be modified to improve speaker privacy while maintaining semantic utility?

- What privacy–utility trade-offs arise when different speaker-related characteristics are anonymized?

## Main concepts

- Interview anonymization
- Speaker identity
- Voice anonymization
- Accent
- Prosody
- Timbre
- Lexical / linguistic style
- Semantic content
- Privacy–utility trade-off
- Speaker identifiability
- Content preservation

## Open questions

- Which anonymization methods should be compared?
- Which representations are most useful for evaluating the effect of anonymization?
- Which datasets are suitable for developing and evaluating the anonymization methods?
- How should semantic content preservation be defined mathematically?
- Which characteristics should be modified, and which should be preserved?
- How should privacy be measured after anonymization?

## Current direction

The main objective of the project is to develop and evaluate anonymization methods for speech interviews.

The different representations of speaker identity, accent, prosody, timbre, linguistic style, and semantic content are used as tools to understand and evaluate the effect of the anonymization methods.

The project will compare different anonymization strategies and measure how much speaker-identifying information remains after transformation, while also evaluating how well the semantic content of the original interview is preserved.
